Shelf hygiene flags

01

Walmart search puts 3.8-star listings above your heroes

A third-party bundle (3.8★, 119 reviews) and a "Restored" unit (3.8★, 398 reviews) rank in the top five results for "ninja creami," above the 4.5-star NC301. First impression damage on the retailer with your biggest review volume.

02

The $149 Walmart CREAMi undercuts every other channel by 24 to 35 percent

If it is a planned rollback, Amazon and Target shoppers are seeing a worse deal on the same franchise entry point. If it is not planned, it is a price-integrity gap worth a conversation with the marketplace team.

How this was built

One agentic pipeline, public data only, built in an afternoon with Claude Code.

01

Collect

Pulled all 1,944 customer reviews (full text, rating, date) for six 2024 to 2026 SharkNinja launches from Best Buy's public review API, plus live price, rating, and rank data from Amazon, Walmart, and Target product and search pages, and SharkNinja.com first-party pricing.

02

Classify

Clustered every negative review (3 stars or less) into complaint themes with a transparent keyword rubric. Every count on this page can be re-derived from the raw corpus; nothing is a black box.

03

Compare

Cross-checked Best Buy sentiment against Amazon rating histograms (5,571 ratings on Deluxe alone, 9 percent 1-star) and Walmart ratings to separate product problems from channel-experience problems.

04

Recommend

The same pipeline, run weekly inside SharkNinja, becomes an early-warning system: safety-language detection routed to quality triage, review-response queues for Amazon, and automated price-integrity alerts. That is the agentic workflow this co-op role describes.

Limits, stated plainly

Review text depth comes from one retailer (Best Buy); Amazon and Walmart contribute headline stats, not full text. Prices are a point-in-time snapshot, and promotional rollbacks can explain part of any spread. Theme counts use keyword matching, so phrasing outside the rubric is missed. All of this is fixable with internal data access.

Recommendations

01

Close the Amazon experience gap

Same machines rate 4.7 at Best Buy and 4.3 on Amazon, where 5 to 10 times more shoppers look first. Reliability and return friction, not taste, drive 41 percent of negatives. A review-response and returns-experience push on Amazon is the highest-leverage star you can buy back.

02

Auto-triage safety language

Reviews like "started smoking and melted through plastic" should reach the quality team the day they post, not whenever someone reads page 14. A classifier on the review feed does this for every SKU, every day.

03

Run a standing price-integrity sweep

Scoop & Swirl holds a $0.99 spread across channels. The base CREAMi and SLUSHi do not ($80.99 and $100.99). The discipline exists; the monitoring to enforce it everywhere is a small agent away.
